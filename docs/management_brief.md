# Management brief

> **Independent portfolio project.** This brief uses public/open data and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, commissioned by, or an official publication of Vancouver Coastal Health.

## Interpretation framework

- **Observed data:** 2016 CHSA population and age counts, Long-Form GNR, ODHF facility records, and authoritative geographic boundaries.
- **Proxy measures:** age-structure vulnerability and facility-location supply gap; neither is a clinical-capacity or healthcare-need measure.
- **Scenario assumptions:** priority-component weights, population/priority allocation weights, and the illustrative resource pools.
- **Hypothetical allocations:** reconciled model outputs for comparative decision support, not actual VCH resources or recommendations.

## Decision context

The analysis prioritizes where a hypothetical incremental resource pool could receive further investigation. It does not estimate current VCH capacity or recommend actual allocations.

### Finding 1: Priority depends on more than one signal

**Observed data and scenario output:** Under the Balanced scenario, Sunset ranks first, followed by West Point Grey/Dunbar-Southlands, Oakridge/Marpole, Hastings-Sunrise, and Northeast False Creek.

**Analytical interpretation:** The combination of age structure, census uncertainty, and low relative facility counts yields a different order than any single component.

**Limitation:** Scores are relative percentile ranks based on limited public data. Facility counts do not equal capacity.

**Management follow-up:** Compare the top-ranked CHSAs with current utilization, wait-time, workforce, service-hours, and referral data before any decision.

### Finding 2: Data uncertainty is a separate planning problem

**Observed data:** Downtown Eastside has the highest Long-Form GNR in the included source at 13.5%.

**Analytical interpretation:** Census-based estimates for that CHSA warrant stronger local validation and community engagement.

**Limitation:** High GNR does not prove socioeconomic vulnerability or healthcare need.

**Management follow-up:** Triangulate with community partner knowledge, program data, and contemporary population estimates.

### Finding 3: Geographic facility presence is uneven

**Observed data:** ODHF point-in-polygon mapping finds 153 canonical facility records inside Vancouver City, spanning 103 nursing/residential care, 33 ambulatory, and 17 hospital records. Northeast False Creek has no mapped ODHF facility under the conservative method.

**Analytical interpretation:** Some areas have lower facility-location density relative to population.

**Limitation:** ODHF coverage is not a census of VCH capacity; a zero can reflect data coverage or boundary effects and does not establish facility ownership.

**Management follow-up:** Replace the supply proxy with an authoritative service inventory containing ownership, programs, operating hours, capacity, staffing, and utilization.

### Finding 4: Rankings are scenario-sensitive

**Scenario output:** Spearman rank correlation versus Balanced ranges from 0.869 to 0.918 for alternative scenarios.

**Analytical interpretation:** Weight choices matter enough to change ordering, even though the overall pattern is moderately stable.

**Limitation:** Weights are subjective scenario assumptions rather than official policy.

**Management follow-up:** Use a facilitated weighting exercise and publish results under multiple scenarios instead of selecting a single “correct” score.

## Allocation interpretation

The model reconciles hypothetical pools of $10,000,000, 50.0 clinical FTE, and 1,000 community-care units using 60% population share and 40% resource-priority share. These figures are illustrative scenario units only. They are not observed resources, approved budgets, staffing plans, operational commitments, or recommendations.
